1. The Serious Problem of Road Accidents

Section 1 of the AEOL textbook – causes, statistics, where/when accidents happen, and vulnerable groups.

1.1 A Real Tragedy – Accident Costs

Traffic accidents are one of the main causes of mortality and injuries. They also have an enormous economic impact which, directly or indirectly, we all assume.

Material damageSanitary damagesAdministrative damagesHuman damages
Vehicles, road, environment, etc.First aid, care for the wounded, etc.Police and firemen, legal costs, etc.Loss of productive capacity, physical and psychological suffering, etc.

1.2 Causes of Road Traffic Accidents

👤 Human factor
70–90%

The main responsible. Mitigating driver's risk factors (distractions, speed, fatigue, alcohol) can reduce and avoid tragic consequences.

🛣️ Road & surroundings
10–35%

Present when driving is not conform to road or weather conditions. Responsible for defects is still ultimately the human factor (proper maintenance could avoid most).

🚗 Vehicle factor
4–13%

Most common defects: tyre (most frequent), brakes and lighting, steering, overload or deficient distribution. Vehicles over 10 years old have more accidents.

1.3 Where and When Accidents Are Produced

📍 Most dangerous places
  • More accidents occur on straight roads than on bends
  • Most fatalities on interurban roads
  • Motorways and dual carriageways are safer than conventional highways
🕐 Most dangerous moments
  • Hours: entering or leaving working places
  • Days: weekends and public holidays, especially at dawn
  • During holidays especially Easter, summer and Christmas
Exam fact

More accidents occur on straight roads than bends – but most fatalities occur on interurban roads. These are often confused in exam questions.

1.4 Most Vulnerable Groups

🧑 Young drivers (15–29 years)
Where & when:
  • Urban centres and outskirts – commuting to work/leisure
  • Night and dawn; weekends; summer; Christmas
Why so many accidents:
  • Alcohol and drugs consumption
  • Disrespect for rules (especially speed limits)
  • Lack of experience; taking too many risks
  • Danger not properly evaluated
👴 Older drivers

More accidents in complex situations requiring a quick answer: junctions, joining/leaving lanes where traffic is speeding. Caused mainly by physical and mental deterioration (less attention, eye and ear conditions, reaction time increased, pill consumption).

🏍️ Moped and motorcycle operators

Accidents increasingly deadly if use of the helmet is overlooked. In the event of a fall, the driver will slide casually along the ground to minimise injury. To avoid accidents: check no vehicles in opposite direction, respect right of way, do not ride parallel, keep proper safe distance, be cautious in bad weather.

🚲 Cyclists

More accidents at weekends on conventional roads (straight stretches and intersections). Most serious injuries are to the head. Most frequent accident: front-lateral collision with another vehicle. Main causes: violations by cyclists AND other drivers. Be careful: when overtaking, near stationary vehicles/obstacles, in built-up areas during summer, in bad weather, do not use acoustic signals near them.

🚶 Pedestrians

More accidents in the city (more than half of deaths from being run over). Fewer accidents on the road but more serious due to high speed. High degree of uncertainty in behaviour. Precautions: slow down near stationary buses, pedestrian crossings, school zones, taking car out from garage (especially ramps), before stationary vehicle ahead (doors may be open), before distracted pedestrians (mobile phone), near built-up/leisure areas in summer, under adverse weather (pedestrians more unpredictable). Do not signal pedestrians to cross (other drivers may not have seen your signals). Be careful reversing. Do not park over the kerb.

👵 Elderly pedestrians

More accidents when walking alone than with children. Difficulties: excessive noise (can't hear vehicles), difficulty perceiving vehicle speed and traffic light colours, lack of pedestrian crossings, poor street lighting, time needed to cross wide streets, kerb height, orientation problems in unfamiliar streets.

👶 Children pedestrians
  • 40% less visual field when walking through parked cars
  • Under 7 years: cannot figure out vehicle direction from sound
  • Under 10 years: get distracted very easily
  • Assume that if they see a car, the driver has seen them
  • Ignorance of rules, signs, proper behaviours
2. The Human Factor – Speed & Fatigue

Sections 2.1 and 2.2 of the textbook. Heavy exam content – know every number.

2.1 Speed

Negative effects of increasing speed
  • Visual field is reduced → tunnel effect (eye perception reduced to centre lane). Extremely dangerous at junctions and when changing lanes.
  • Ability to capture surroundings worsens → ability to anticipate and react is reduced.
  • Injuries from accident are more serious. At 50 km/h the collision energy = falling from a 3rd floor.
  • After a while on large roads you tend to think you're travelling slower than you actually are.
  • Driving too long at high speed → increases tension, accelerates fatigue, leads to hostile/aggressive behaviour.

2.2 Fatigue and Sleepiness

Key statistic

Driving in a state of sleepiness produces between 15 and 30% of road accidents, usually very serious. You do not have to fall asleep – risks appear long before that. Does not only happen at night.

😴 Effects of fatigue
  • Muscular stiffness – increases movements in seat
  • Slowness and lack of precision in movements
  • Decrease of attention, concentration, ability to receive stimuli (prone to errors)
  • Change of humour (irritation, takes more risks)
  • Reduction of the ability to react (reaction time increased)
⚠️ Signs of sleepiness at the wheel
  • Reaction time increase (making decisions takes longer)
  • Monotonous atmospheres and low traffic flow → distractions
  • Errors increase, especially in complex situations
  • Slow, imprecise and thoughtless movements
  • Micro sleep (brief episodes with no recollection)
  • Eye strain, blurred vision, dazzling
  • Different moods: restlessness, hostility, greater risk acceptance
🔧 Factors contributing to fatigue
Driver related
  • Lack of experience
  • Driving long periods without proper rest
  • Adopting bad postures
  • Over-speeding for long periods
  • Driving sleepy, under alcohol or sick
  • Change in driving habits
Road related
  • Heavy congested roads
  • Damaged road surface
  • Unknown roads (require attentiveness)
  • Adverse weather (hard driving)
Vehicle related
  • Faulty maintenance (suspension, steering, lighting)
  • Poor ventilation or high temperature (must be 20–23°C, air not blown at eyes)
  • Poor ergonomic seats and interior
💡 To mitigate sleep driving, consider
  • Moment of day: dawn (3–5am) and early afternoon (14–16)
  • Changes in sleep habits, especially in shift work
  • Sleep quality AND quantity (a bad sleep = dangerous as a short sleep)
  • Monotony: be active while night driving on motorways
  • Sedative and stimulant substances: alcohol and medicines can produce sleepiness. Stimulants (tea, coffee) may have a rebound effect
  • Abundant food: induces sedative effect
  • Interior temperature and ventilation: hot temperatures may set off somnolence
Recommendations
  • Whenever tired or sleepy: pull over in a quiet place and take a nap
  • Take a rest at least 20–30 minutes every 2 hours or 200 km
  • Take fatigue and sleep symptoms very seriously
2. Human Factor – Substances & Distractions

Sections 2.3–2.8: Illnesses, medicines, heat, distractions, alcohol, drugs and legal detection.

2.3 Illnesses and Medicines

🤧 Allergies

Symptoms similar to a cold: miscalculates distances, reduces concentration (especially during sneezing fit). Antihistamines → heavy somnolence. Advice: don't open windows, keep AC low, clean ventilation ducts, never drink alcohol while taking medicines, don't do long trips without doctor's advice, wear sunglasses, avoid driving at dawn and in moist zones, don't self-medicate.

😤 Stress effects on driver
  • Higher level of aggressiveness, hostility and competitiveness
  • Behaviour turns increasingly imprudent
  • Tendency to disrespect rules and signs
  • Slower decision making / poor judgement
  • Higher level of distracted conduct
  • Higher level of fatigue
😔 Depression effects on driver
  • Higher level of distracted behaviour
  • Loss of perception of surroundings
  • More prone to sleepiness and fatigue
  • Higher levels of exasperation, anxiety and insecurity
  • Associated with prescribed drugs consumption
💊 Prescribed drugs
  • Always ask doctor or pharmacist's advice on risks
  • Use them properly (no self-medication, no mix with alcohol)
  • Read the prescription form – check effects on driving
  • Follow doctor's prescription (frequency and dosage)
  • Don't drive or be very prudent at the beginning of treatment
  • Don't mix medicines not prescribed by doctor
  • Be aware of coffee and other substances changing effects

2.4 Heat

Driving under hot weather conditions affects driver abilities dangerously: needs more time to react, becomes vulnerable to errors, hot weather increases fatigue, sleep and distractions, as well as hostility and aggressiveness.

Recommendations
  • Stop as much as you can and preferably not under the sun
  • Drink as much water or juices; avoid abundant meals; be extremely careful after each meal
  • Wear light clothes and sunglasses
  • Switch on air conditioning at an average temperature of 20 to 23°C

2.5 Distractions

When distraction accidents are most frequent
  • Among young drivers 18–25 years or aged over 70
  • While driving in company of others (friends, family)
  • During summer season, weekends, in daylight and leisure trips
  • In motorways and dual carriageways for their monotony
👤 Internal causes (driver)
  • Attitudes requiring your attention (GPS, map, looking for a direction)
  • Fatigue and sleepiness
  • Stress, anxiety and/or depression
  • Old age
  • Alcohol, drugs or prescribed drugs
  • Certain personality characteristics
  • Use of mobile phone or radio
  • Being absorbed by thoughts or discussions
  • Turning head back to talk or play with children
  • Throw insects out of car
  • Eating, drinking, smoking
  • Looking at rear-view mirror
🌆 External causes
  • Familiar roads
  • Low street lights and sudden changes in lighting (tunnels, glare)
  • Complex situations (many road signs, vehicles, pedestrians)
  • Partially concealed signs or locations on edges of visual field
📱 Mobile phone – risk equivalent to 1 g/l alcohol (4× bigger risk)
Effects while using phone (incl. hands-free):
  • Attention diminished (looking, manipulating, talking)
  • More infractions: after 90s, 40% of road signs overlooked
  • Distances miscalculated, safe distance shortened
  • Speed miscalculated: after 90s speed drops ~12%
  • Trajectory deviations (most frequent: swerving off road)
  • You become disorientated → wrong and dangerous decisions
Most dangerous moments:
Receiving a callTexting messagesTalkingHanging up the phone
Recommendations:
  • Be aware if phone is on – sounds can catch you off guard
  • Do not use phone: find a safe place to stop
  • If hands-free: tell partner you're driving; keep under 1 minute
🗺️ GPS navigators
  • Plan your trip before starting (do not navigate while driving)
  • Listen to voice instructions (don't look at screen)
  • Let the driver's reasoning prevail at all times
  • Main source of information = speedometer (not GPS)
  • Proper support: high enough, don't look away from road, not close to airbag
  • Cable arrangement: steering wheel and transmission must not be hindered
🚬 Smoking
  • Lose ability to operate vehicle or focus while lighting or smoking
  • Underestimate traffic situations → more risks
  • Carbon monoxide in tobacco reduces concentration and creates vision disturbances
  • Carbon monoxide poisoning → severe medical conditions, especially cardiovascular

Critical exam point

Even driving with a level below the maximum allowed, you can suffer a road accident with origin on alcohol effects. The only level actually safe is 0.0 g/l. From July 2022, buses with more than 9 seats must be equipped with immobilising breathalyser interface.

🍷 What affects blood alcohol level
Driver's inherent characteristics:
  • Weight: thin people reach higher levels
  • Sex: women generally reach higher blood alcohol levels
  • Age: under 18 and over 65 are more vulnerable
  • Personal circumstances: fatigue, sleep, disabilities, etc.
How you take it:
  • Amount taken = most important factor
  • Time elapsed: detected after 5 min; peak after 30–90 min
  • Frequency: time between drinks allows liver to metabolise
  • Time of day: eliminated more slowly during sleeping hours
  • Type: fermentation (beer/wine) absorbed slower; distillation (gin/rum/whisky) faster; sparkling/hot beverages stimulate level
  • Empty stomach: alcohol reaches bloodstream faster
NOT effective to reduce blood alcohol

Physical activity, chewing gum or candies, drinking or chewing coffee, ingesting olive oil, smoking, cocaine intake, mouth sprays, drinking significant amounts of water, eating egg white.

2.7 Drugs

Key statistic

Around 10% of serious road accidents are directly or indirectly related to drugs. No driver is allowed under the effects of narcotics, psychoactive drugs, stimulants, pharmaceutical drugs or any substance altering physical or psychological condition to drive.

Cannabis
Alters perception (surroundings, time, space). Reduces concentration. Increases sleepiness and reaction time.
Cocaine
Alters perception, produces aggressiveness/impulsiveness. Competence overestimated; risks better tolerated.
Ecstasy (MDMA)
Visual illusion, dazzling, blurred vision. Reduces concentration. Episodic depression/anxiety. Physical/mental exhaustion after effects disappear. Flashbacks.
LSD
Strong distortion and hallucinations. Emotional alterations (aggressiveness, anxiety, panic). Effects last up to 10–12 hours. Flashbacks occur.
Amphetamines
Impatience, impulsiveness, violence. False self-confidence. Repetitive movements limiting mobility. Sense of fatigue decreases (producing exhaustion). Delays sleep-phase → irrepressible need for sleeping (rebound effect).

2.8 Legal Detection of Alcohol and/or Drug Consumption

🔬 Who must be tested
  • All road users directly involved as possible responsible for accidents
  • All road users, cyclists included, upon Law Enforcement Officers' request

3. The Vehicle Factor

Sections 3.1–3.3: Driver's seat, vehicle controls, and all safety components (active, passive, preventive).

3.1 Accommodation and Adjustments in the Driver's Seat

Why it matters

A correct posture facilitates a better and faster reaction in view of an unpredictable event.

Car seat position
Feet should press the pedals with legs slightly bent.
Backrest and steering wheel
Arms stretched forward; back flat against backrest; wrists should reach the upper steering wheel (arms slightly bent).
Height
Seat and steering wheel height enough to enable the driver to see above the wheel.

Also adjust: headrestrear-view mirrorsseatbelt

Seat too close to steering wheel

Produces fatigue, restrains mobility (forced and slow movements), particularly dangerous in vehicles with airbag devices.

Seat too far from steering wheel

Delays reaction speed and promptness, compromising the effectiveness of security devices.

3.2 Vehicle Controls

🦶 Feet operated
  • Clutch – left foot
  • Service brake – right foot
  • Accelerator – right foot
✋ Hand operated
  • Steering wheel
  • Parking brake
  • Gearshift

Hands grip outer steering wheel at quarter to three position.

🏍️ Motorcycle controls
  • Gearbox – left foot
  • Clutch lever – left hand
  • Rear brake – right foot
  • Accelerator – right hand
  • Front brake – right hand

3.3.1 Active (Primary) Safety – Tyres

Tyres – most important component

Poor condition of tyres represents the main cause of road accidents attributed to mechanical failure. The vehicle loses grip and brakes worse. This component requires the most attention of all.

Under-inflated
  • Worn out on the sidewalls
  • Become deformed and heat faster
  • May burst
  • Grip decreases; hydroplaning risk increases
  • Fuel consumption increases
Over-inflated
  • Worn out on the centre
  • Tread reduced; grip reduced
  • Road irregularities not well absorbed (more vibrations; suspension deteriorates)
Unbalanced pressure
  • Each wheel adheres and wears differently
  • Vehicle likely to turn to under-inflated side
  • Braking more irregular (deviation more pronounced)
Tubeless tyre
Air stored in assembly formed by wheel and tyre. If punctured, loses air slowly. Safest type.
Tube type
Air stored in chamber inside. Less safe than tubeless.
Radial tyre
Frame has ropes and wires placed in the form of spokes.
When to change tyres
  • When tread grooves depth is about 1.6 mm (you should change before this)
  • Check date of manufacture: after 5 years a tyre may have lost its properties
  • Tyres of the same construction must be fitted into all wheels (or at least per steerable axle)
  • Never fit tyres with inferior load or speed ratings to what the manufacturer recommends
Tyre burst – what to do
  • Keep calm; maintain hands firmly on steering wheel and correct possible route deviations
  • Do NOT release the accelerator at once
  • Brake as little as possible and very smoothly

  • If wheels about to lock up: reduce brake pedal pressure

  • Step on brake pedal thoroughly
  • Maintain maximum pressure, even if vibrations or noise are produced
  • ABS avoids blockade of wheels in abrupt braking
Brake failure – what to do
  1. Pump the brake pedal
  2. If over-heated: release pedal and let them air
  3. Keep driving close to right-hand side and engage lower gears (use motor brake)
  4. If needed: pull handbrake but in a smooth and progressive way
  5. If still unable to stop: find emergency brake bay. If not: find emergency exit preferably into an upward hill

3.3.2 Passive Safety – Seat Belt

Retains body in case of collision or overturning, avoids physical injury or being thrown out. Reduces chances of being killed by nearly a half.

Correct positioning:
  • Shoulder strap over collarbone and across chest
  • Lap strap over pelvis and hip bones
  • Tight against body – do not use a clip
  • Avoid voluminous outerwear
  • Effective during pregnancy
  • Must replace after previous accident
Submarine effect:

During accident, body slips below lap strap as seat belt unable to restrain. To avoid: don't add towels/cushions/covers to seat; adjust seat belt and seat properly; don't recline excessively or drive lying down.

3.3.2 Passive Safety – Child Seats, Airbags, Headrests

👶 SRIR – Child Restraint System
  • Central rear seat is safest (least affected in side impacts)
  • Old models: grouped by child's weight
  • Current i-Size models: by height range (i-Size)
  • i-Size system includes top tether or support leg + ISOFIX
  • Travel in opposite direction until at least 15 months or 83 cm
  • Deactivate frontal passenger airbag if placing rear-facing baby seat
💥 Air bags
  • Avoid direct impact of occupants against vehicle components
  • Airbag is complementary to seat belt – not a substitute
  • Allow 25 cm distance from steering wheel
  • Wear seat belt
  • Deactivate frontal passenger airbag for rear-facing baby seat
  • Keep area clear of objects
  • Never allow passenger feet on dashboard
🪑 Headrest

Prevents cervical injuries from whiplash especially in rear-end accidents.

  • Height: upper part of headrest = upper part of head (broader part in line with occupant's eyes)
  • Distance: as minimal as possible (never over 4 cm)

3.3.2 Passive Safety – Motorcycle Equipment

🪖 Helmet
  • Must be homologated (passed safety tests)
  • Full face helmets = most recommended
  • Polycarbonate helmets: don't last long; no paints or stickers
  • Size: well adjusted to head
  • Visor: preferably anti-fog
  • Buckle claps: prevent accidental throwing off
  • Colour: light, bright, reflecting = safest
  • Well ventilated = more comfortable and safe
🧥 Other equipment
  • Jacket/suit: leather or abrasion-resistant fabric; armour protection; colour should attract attention at distance
  • Footwear: protect feet, closed and tight-fitting; heel allows adequate motorcycle handling
  • Gloves: protect from cold and in event of falling over

3.3.3 Preventive Safety – Mirrors

Cars, vans, lorries up to 2 tons MAM must have:
  • Left wing mirror (category III)
  • Rear-view mirror (category I): see at least 60m behind and 20m side to side
  • If rear window covered or towing trailer/luggage: two wing mirrors (one each side)
Motorcycles (category L):
  • Speed ≤100 km/h: at least one on left side
  • Speed >100 km/h: one on each side
  • Two-wheeled mopeds: one side mirror on left handlebar
  • Three-wheeled/quadricycle without body covering: left and right rearview mirror
Blind spot

An area that cannot be seen from the rear and sides of the vehicle, even if all mirrors are well regulated. Always check blind spot physically when changing lanes or turning.

3.4 Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems (ADAS)

Section 3.4 of the textbook. ADAS use sensors and smart cameras to improve active safety. The driver retains full responsibility even when ADAS is active.

Key rule

ADAS can be disconnected permanently or temporarily by the driver, who must have absolute control of the vehicle. Their use does NOT exempt the driver from responsibility for causing an accident or dangerous situation.

Systems that count in the driving test marking

TPMS
Tyre Pressure Monitoring System
Evaluates tyre pressure or variation and transmits info to the user while vehicle is in motion.
AEB
Advanced Emergency Braking
Detects imminent rear-end danger; issues visual/acoustic warning; performs emergency braking if driver doesn't respond.
AUTO
Auto lighting & wipers
Activated when sensor detects decrease in visibility or presence of rain. If sensor defective, system cannot perform its function.
VIS-DET
Pedestrian & cyclist collision warning
Sends visual and audio alert in event of imminent collision with a pedestrian or cyclist.
AEB-PCD
Advanced Pedestrian & Cyclist Emergency Braking
Detects emergency situation with pedestrians/cyclists and applies vehicle brakes to avoid or mitigate collision.
RCTA
Rear Cross Traffic Alert
Monitors rear traffic crosswise when backing out. If collision estimated within 2–3.5s, emits warning. May apply maximum braking force if speed below 15 km/h.
DDR
Fatigue / Drowsiness Detection
Alerts driver via text message, steaming cup image, or steering wheel vibrations if losing concentration due to fatigue/sleepiness.
ADR
Advanced Driver Distraction Warning
Facial monitoring via cameras detects gaze deviations implying inattention; warns driver when distracted.
BSIS-BSM
Blind Spot Monitoring
Informs of possible collision in blind spot via optical/acoustic warnings. May act on steering or stop vehicle.
LDW
Lane Departure Warning
Monitors lane position; warns visually, acoustically or by vibration if vehicle leaves lane unintentionally. If defective: driver warned camera cannot function.
LKA
Advanced Lane Keeping System
Maintains safe position in lane/road. Combined with LDW which acts in advance when vehicle leaves or is about to leave.
HILL
Slope Exit Support (Hill Holder)
Detects inclination angle; prevents rolling backwards when foot removed from brake. Some allow keeping vehicle stopped on slope.
REV / 360°
Reversing Camera & Parking Sensors
Improve vision and perception of surroundings when parking/reversing. Parking sensors emit light/sound alert detecting objects behind without intervening in steering or braking. 360° simulates zenithal view.
EBD
Emergency Braking Display
Rapid flashing of brake lights when brakes applied powerfully and rapidly in emergency. Helps driver behind detect emergency braking ahead.
SLI / ISA
Speed Limiting Device / Intelligent Speed Assist
Controls fuel flow to limit vehicle speed to specified value. Helps driver maintain appropriate speed for road environment.
EDR
Incidence Data Recorder (Black Box)
Records and stores critical parameters and information related to a collision, shortly before, during and immediately after.

Systems allowed in test but NOT counted in marking

Start-Stop
Start-Stop System
Switches engine off when idling; back on when clutch depressed. Reduces CO2 and saves fuel.
TSR
Traffic Signal Recognition
Reads speed limits/overtaking prohibition signs via sensor/camera at rear of interior mirror.
AUTO-L
Automatic Lights
When >50 km/h or out of town, automatically controls road lights via camera/sensor to avoid dazzling others.
Cannot be deactivated = NOT eligible for skills tests

Traffic Signal Recognition and Automatic Lights systems shall not be eligible for the skills and behavioural tests if they cannot be deactivated.

Adherence depends on the condition of the vehicle itself AND the type and condition of the road surface.

4.2 Bends

Centripetal force
Consequence of the driver's actions to perform cornering. Works inward.
Centrifugal force (most dangerous)
Pushes the vehicle outwards while performing the curve. Increases as: curve sharpens (reduced radius), speed increases, vehicle is heavier.
Good cornering technique (4 steps)
  1. Apply the brakes before you initiate the curve and engage a gear that allows suitable acceleration
  2. Turn the steering wheel with precision but smoothly
  3. Accelerate gently but sustainably when exiting the curve
  4. Unwind softly the steering wheel without letting it loose

Permanent deviation panels show how dangerous the curve is: multiple panels = increasing danger; single panel = informing of design only.

4.3 Skidding

Skidding is produced by lack of adherence of the tyres

Most frequent causes: inadequate use of vehicle controls (main cause); excessive speed combined with worn/under-inflated tyres, poor shock absorbers/brakes, and a slippery surface.

⚠️ Front skidding – traction vehicles
Wheel locking (no response to steering)
Stop applying the brakes at once.
Too much acceleration
Stop accelerating.
Over-speeding
Accelerate smoothly and unwind the steering wheel until the vehicle recuperates adherence.
⚠️ Rear axle skidding – traction vehicles
Step 1
Accelerate gently – do NOT apply the brakes nor the clutch.
Step 2 (counter steering)
Turn the steering wheel very gently to the same side that the rear wheels skid.

4.4 Adverse Weather Conditions

☀️ Sun
  • Select the most convenient hour: avoid driving between sunset and sunrise
  • Keep the windows clean and adjust the sun visor
  • Wear sunglasses (note: in night driving, sunglasses are forbidden)
💨 Wind

Especially dangerous blowing sideways or in gusts. Risk of losing control and overturning. Particularly dangerous for motorcycles, mopeds, large vehicles and those pulling trailers.

  • Slow down speed
  • Keep hands firmly on wheel and pull AGAINST the wind direction (blowing left to right → pull left)
  • Careful when passing a shield object (tall buildings, mountains, tunnels, large vehicles) – they can generate gusts of opposite directions
🌧️ Rain

Produces decrease in visibility and adherence. Road surface becomes slippery at the first rain drops.

  • Keep moderate speed and double the safety distance
  • Brake sooner and smoothly (braking distance may double)
  • Check brake efficiency after flooded section (apply brakes several times to dry them)
  • At high speed: risk of hydroplaning (grooves can't disperse water → lose grip)
  • Motorcycle: keep perpendicular to ground; decrease speed in sliding areas, avoid sudden acceleration/braking; bends: don't lean too much and open angle of rotation
🌫️ Fog
  • Extremely careful near rivers or humid zones and in presence of cyclists
  • Switch on headlights; keep windscreen clean and interior ventilated
  • Increase safety distance and adapt speed to vision field
  • Do NOT overtake unless you can see clearly
  • Stop driving if visibility is poor
❄️ Snow

Especially slippery when first snowflakes fall. Even more dangerous when it hardens (same effect as ice).

  • Drive in highest gear possible (2nd or 3rd gear)
  • Moving off: wheels straight, release clutch gently, accelerate smoothly/steadily
  • Cornering: keep steady speed, avoid braking
  • Uphill: drive slowly and at a steady speed
  • Downhill: lowest gear; descend slowly; avoid brakes (use motor brake)
  • Avoid sudden steering; change gears smoothly
  • Slow down and increase safety distance; guide through ruts left by other vehicles if snow is abundant
  • On hard snow or ice: do not accelerate nor brake
🧊 Ice – adherence virtually non-existent
Effects:
  • Wheels do not respond to steering wheel turn (too soft)
  • Braking distance may be up to 10 times bigger
Find ice more often:
  • At night or at dawn
  • Outside temperature around or below 0°C
  • Dark areas (near trees, mountains, under bridges)
  • Humid areas, especially at night
  • On bridges, flyovers, subways and ditches
Icy windows:
  • Direct AC at the glass
  • Switch on rear window demister
  • Scrub with a non-scratching scraper
⛓️ Devices to improve traction (snow/ice)
Snow chains (steel or textile fibre):
  • Both types must be removed when they are not necessary
Special tyres:
  • For mud and snow: must have M+S, M&S or MS inscription
  • With metal studs: round-cornered, cannot stand out more than 2 mm
  • Fit when temperature drops below 7°C approximately
Where to mount them

All traction devices (chains or special tyres) must be mounted at least on the driving wheels – those receiving torque from the engine.
