Obligation under the Penal Code

Providing and getting help are OBLIGATIONS. Their disregard is considered an offence of neglect to providing help — punishable by law. A driver who leaves the scene after causing an accident in which people died may receive imprisonment from 6 months to 4 years and deprivation of the right to drive from 1 to 4 years.

P — Protect: Securing the Scene
Protect yourself FIRST — do not become a victim

Scene safety actions

Park in a safe place before joining the sceneDo not put yourself in danger
If you must walk on the roadWear reflective waistcoat (chaleco) and be extra careful
Mark the accident withEmergency light AND luminous V-16 danger pre-signalling device
Smoking or starting a fireFORBIDDEN — fuel leaks create explosion/fire risk
Vehicle on fire — extinguisherAim at the BASE of the fire — NEVER at the flames directly
No extinguisher availableUse blankets, mud, or sand — NEVER water on a vehicle fire

Rules for injured persons

Do NOT do these
Move injured unless absolutely necessary · Remove motorcyclist's helmet unless necessary and familiar with technique · Give food, drink, or medication · Make them walk
Permitted exceptions to not moving
Risk of fire · Cardiac arrest requiring CPR · Need to put in recovery position. In all cases: treat as a stiff block + 3 bystanders minimum + head/neck/torso aligned.
Person on fire

Use a blanket to smother flames or make them roll on the ground. Fire extinguisher: do NOT aim directly at the person's body — aim at the base. NEVER use water on a vehicle fire.

A — Alert: Calling 112

3 types of information to provide

1
Location: Road, number, driver location sign (pk), community of reference.
2
Injured: Number of injured, their condition, distinguishing features (trapped, cliff risk, etc.).
3
Your identity: Name, identity card number, and phone number — to enable verification and follow-up.

Co-operation with police

  • Produce all documentation (vehicle AND personal)
  • Facilitate all information about the accident
  • Undergo detection tests for alcohol, narcotics, psychoactive drugs, stimulants, and other substances
S — Succour: ABC Assessment
First priority: unresponsive · breathing problems · massive blood loss · vomiting

Immediate actions for ALL injured

  • Loosen clothes around neck and waist
  • Make them warm — even in summer
  • Do NOT give food, drink, or medication
  • Do NOT make them walk

ABC Primary Assessment

A
Airway — forehead-chin manoeuvre: Two fingers under the chin, lift slightly. Other hand on forehead, gently tilt head upward. Remove mouth objects with hooked index finger. Most common cause of obstruction: the tongue.
B
Breathing: Look (chest rise/fall) · Feel (expired air on cheek) · Listen (airflow through mouth/nose). Noisy/hard = partially obstructed. Shallow/irregular with interruptions = risk of brain damage.
C
Circulation: If still not breathing → assume cardiac arrest → perform CPR immediately. Check carotid pulse (index + middle fingers, under the jaw, one side of neck) or radial pulse (external wrist).
Normal heart rate — healthy adults60 to 80 times per minute
Normal heart rate — childrenApproximately 100 times per minute
Accident victims' heart rateHigher than normal — caused by stress and agitation
CPR — Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation
For a NON-BREATHING unresponsive person. Ratio: 30 compressions : 2 rescue breaths

Step 1 — Chest compressions (×30)

1
Lay victim on a firm surface, face up. Kneel at chest height.
2
Place heel of one hand on the CENTRE of the chest. Other hand on top — interlock fingers.
3
Arms straight. Use body weight. Push down 4 or 5 cm — do NOT bend arms. Lift hands slightly after each compression. Repeat 30 times.

Step 2 — Rescue breaths (×2)

1
Keep forehead-chin lift. Pinch the nose. Seal lips around victim's mouth.
2
Exhale for about 1 second — watch for chest rise. Remove mouth to let chest fall. If no chest rise: confirm forehead-chin lift is correct and mouth is not obstructed.
3
If victim does not breathe: immediately repeat 30 compressions. Continue 30:2 cycle.
CPR cadence30:2 — continuous
Stop CPR whenEmergency trained personnel arrives OR victim resumes breathing
Two rescuers presentAlternate every 1–2 minutes to avoid fatigue
CPR for children — key differences

Start with 5 ventilations (mouth-to-mouth or mouth-to-mouth-nose). Compress to approximately 1/3 of chest depth. For children under 1 year old: use two fingers for compressions (not the heel of the hand).

Recovery Position (PLS) & Internal Bleeding
For an UNCONSCIOUS person who IS BREATHING — to prevent suffocation

The side position keeps the mouth downward, allowing the tongue to fall forward and fluids (vomit, blood) to drain from the airway.

When MUST the recovery position be used?

  • When vomiting or bleeding from the mouth or ear
  • When the victim must be left alone to seek help

3-step technique

1
Bend one arm upwards and place the other arm's hand over the shoulder of the arm bent.
2
Bend the leg on the same side and roll the body onto that side — rescuer sustains the head.
3
Keep the leg bent and lean the head on the hand or forearm.

Externalized internal bleeding — positions

SourcePosition
Bleeding from the EAR (possible skull base fracture)Recovery position over the BLEEDING EAR. Cover with sterile gauze WITHOUT blocking.
Bleeding from NOSE (possible skull fracture) — unconsciousRecovery position
Bleeding from MOUTH — coughing blood (lung lesion)HALF-SEATED position
Bleeding from MOUTH — vomiting blood (stomach)Recovery position
⛔ NEVER BLOCK externalized internal bleeding

These bleedings are the result of an extremely serious situation (possible skull fractures). Blocking them causes dangerous internal pressure buildup.

Bleeding Control, Shock, Fractures, Wounds & Burns

Controlling severe haemorrhage — 4 steps

1
Direct pressure on wound with gauze or clean cloth. Do NOT remove even if saturated — add more on top.
2
Lift the limb affected — keep pressure on.
3
Arterial compression — pressure on the major artery feeding the bleeding point.
4
Tourniquet as LAST RESORT above bleeding area. NEVER remove or loosen. Record EXACT TIME applied visibly on victim's clothing.

Shock

Signs of shockPale skin · cold sweat · restlessness · confusion · weak and rapid pulse · rapid shallow breathing
Anti-shock positionSupine (face up) · head LOWER than body · legs ELEVATED
Give something to drink?NEVER
If nausea or vomiting occursIMMEDIATELY place in recovery position

Fractures

  • Cover wound and inhibit bleeding (open fractures)
  • Do NOT reduce (put bone back in place)
  • Immobilise by holding the two extremities

Serious wounds

  • Do NOT touch unless bleeding needs to be reduced
  • Do NOT remove puncturing object — cover with it still embedded
  • Evisceration: lay down + cover organs with MOISTENED cloth

Burns caused by heat

  • Do NOT touch area or remove garment
  • Do NOT puncture or cut blisters
  • Do NOT apply ointment
  • DO apply cold water — submerge if possible
  • Cover with clear MOISTENED dressings + bandage
  • Cover multiple burns SEPARATELY — never together
  • Elevate burnt area if possible

When to use a private vehicle to transport injured

Condition 1
No means available to call for medical help.
Condition 2
Condition deteriorates and medical help has not arrived.
Condition 3
More than 30 minutes since calling AND bleeding severely uncontrolled OR signs of shock.
