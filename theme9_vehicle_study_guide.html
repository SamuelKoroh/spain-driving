1 & 2: The Engine & Its Systems

Understanding how the engine works and its key systems is essential — these topics appear frequently in the theory exam.

What is an engine?

The engine is a machine designed to convert energy into mechanical power (motion). In a car, fuel burns inside cylinders, pushing pistons that ultimately drive the wheels. A 4-stroke petrol cycle: Intake → Compression → Power → Exhaust. Diesel engines omit the spark plug — compression alone ignites the fuel.

2.1 Fuel System

Provides the engine with the correct air-fuel mixture. Comprises two circuits:

Air feeding circuit
Draws in and filters outside air through the air filter before delivering it to the intake manifold.
Fuel feeding circuit
Draws fuel from the tank through the fuel filter and pump to the injectors or carburettor.
Maintenance
  • Check the air filter regularly — it gets dirtier in summer than winter. Replace per manufacturer schedule.
  • Check idling speed — if too fast, fuel consumption and contamination both increase.
  • Replace the fuel filter following the manufacturer's recommendations.

2.2 Exhaust System

Conveys exhaust gases from the engine to the exterior. Comprises two key components:

Muffler (Silenciador)
Reduces noise and lowers gas temperature before release.
Catalytic Converter (Catalizador)
Chemically transforms toxic gases (CO, HC, NOx) into less harmful compounds. Works best at operating temperature.
Warning

A damaged catalytic converter increases harmful emissions and will cause the vehicle to fail its ITV inspection.

2.3 Lubrication System

Distributes thin layers of motor oil around engine components to reduce friction, heat, and wear. Includes an oil pressure gauge with a warning light.

Light OFF

Engine is properly lubricated — normal operation.

Light ON

Oil pressure is below minimum. Stop immediately — serious engine damage can result.

Maintenance
  • Check oil level: vehicle on a flat surface, engine OFF and COLD. Level must be between MIN and MAX on the dipstick.
  • Change oil: vehicle on a flat surface, engine HOT. Drain via the crankcase drain plug (bottom). Refill via the oil filter cap (top/cylinder head).
  • Change the oil filter as often as the manufacturer recommends.

2.4 Cooling System

Draws out part of the heat produced by engine activity to prevent overheating. Uses water-based coolant circulated through the radiator.

Maintenance
  • Check coolant level in the reservoir: engine must be COLD. Level must be between MIN and MAX.
  • Change coolant per manufacturer's instructions — protective effectiveness typically lasts ~2 years.
  • In winter, add antifreeze fluid to prevent coolant from freezing.
Note

Rust on coolant hoses may indicate a loss of coolant — inspect the cooling system carefully.

3: Electrical Equipment

The vehicle's electrical systems power everything from starting to lighting — all are tested in the exam.

3.1 Battery

Supplies electrical power when the engine is motionless (starting, lights with engine off, accessories).

Electrolyte solution
A mixture of distilled water and sulphuric acid. The battery plates must always be fully submerged in this solution.
Battery typeMaintenance required
Low maintenanceCheck electrolyte solution level every 6 months. Add distilled water if plates are exposed.
Free maintenanceNo solution level check needed. However, terminals must always be tight and clean.
Maintenance
  • Check electrolyte level — add distilled water only if plates are exposed.
  • Clean terminals and apply a coat of vaseline or soft grease to prevent corrosion.
  • Keep battery caps clean.

3.2 Charging System

Supplies electrical power when the engine is running. The alternator generates electricity and recharges the battery simultaneously.

Warning

If the charging system dashboard light illuminates while driving, it may indicate an alternator malfunction, broken, or slack belt. Reduce electrical load and drive safely to a garage.

3.3 Starting System

The starter motor prompts the first turns of the engine to initiate combustion.

3.4 Ignition System

Spark plugs trigger a spark inside petrol engines only to ignite the air-fuel mixture. Diesel engines use compression ignition — no spark plugs.

Maintenance

Check and replace spark plugs following the manufacturer's instructions. Worn plugs cause misfires, difficult starting, and increased fuel consumption.

3.5 Lighting System

Enables the driver to see, be seen, and signal manoeuvres. Includes: dipped headlights, full beam, fog lights, direction indicators, hazard lights, rear lights, brake lights, and reversing lights.

Maintenance

Replace any failed bulb as soon as possible — defective lights are a traffic infraction and create genuine safety hazards.

4: Transmission, Steering & Suspension

The transmission transfers engine power to the wheels. Understanding drive types is essential for the exam.

Drive types

⬆
Front-wheel drive

Tracción

Engine torque drives the front wheels, which also steer. Common in small and medium cars. The front wheels both pull and guide the vehicle.

⬇
Rear-wheel drive

Propulsión

Engine torque drives the rear wheels. Better balance under hard acceleration. Common in sports and larger cars.

◎
All-wheel drive (4x4)

4x4

All four wheels receive engine torque. Best traction on slippery or off-road surfaces. Used in SUVs and off-road vehicles.

Gearbox types

TypeFeatures
ManualDriver operates clutch pedal and gear lever. Greater control, generally better fuel efficiency.
AutomaticNo clutch pedal — gears change automatically. Easier in heavy urban traffic.
CVTContinuously Variable Transmission — no fixed gears, seamless power delivery.

Power steering & Suspension

🔄
Power steering (Dirección asistida)

Hydraulic or electric assistance reduces the effort required to steer, particularly at low speeds and when parking.

Maintenance

For hydraulic systems: check power steering fluid level regularly.

〰
Suspension system

Absorbs road irregularities to maintain tyre contact and passenger comfort. Key components: springs, shock absorbers (amortiguadores), and anti-roll bars.

Maintenance

Check for oil leaks or excessive bouncing — worn shock absorbers increase braking distance.

Hydraulic brake system

Pressing the brake pedal pushes brake fluid through pipes to calipers (disc brakes) or wheel cylinders (drum brakes), creating friction to slow the vehicle.

Maintenance
  • Check brake fluid level — must be between MIN and MAX in the reservoir.
  • Brake fluid is hygroscopic (absorbs moisture over time), which lowers its boiling point. Change per manufacturer schedule (typically every 2 years).
  • A spongy pedal may indicate air in the system — requires immediate professional attention.

ABS (Anti-lock Braking System)

Prevents wheel lock during emergency braking, allowing the driver to maintain steering while stopping. ABS does not always shorten stopping distance — its primary benefit is maintaining steerability.

When ABS activates

You feel a pulsating sensation in the brake pedal. This is normal — keep firm, steady pressure and steer to safety. Do NOT release the pedal.

ABS warning light ON

Brakes still work conventionally, but ABS assistance is disabled. Have the system checked at a garage.

Parking brake (Freno de mano)

Acts typically on the rear wheels via a mechanical cable or electric actuator. Must always be applied when parking.

Warning

Never use the handbrake as an emergency brake at speed — it can cause rear-wheel skid and loss of vehicle control.

V-16 Technical requirements
  • Horizontal visibility: 360°; vertical: minimum ±8°
  • Luminous intensity: 40–700 candlepower (grade 0); 25–600 at ±8 grades
  • Protection: at least IP54 (dust and splash resistant)
  • Flash frequency: 0.8–2 Hz
  • Autonomous power supply (from vehicle battery or rechargeable battery)
  • Communicates activation, deactivation, and GPS to national traffic access point every 100 seconds
Spare tyre limits
  • Maximum speed: 80 km/h
  • Maximum travel distance: 50–200 km
  • An alternative tyre system is allowed if it offers similar roadworthy guarantees
