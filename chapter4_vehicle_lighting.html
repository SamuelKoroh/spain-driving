Vehicle Lighting — Purposes & Colour Code

Three purposes: indicate vehicle presence (be seen) · illuminate the road (see and be seen) · warn other users (signal and be seen).

Colour Code — Quick Reference

ColourUsed for
WHITEFront sidelights, dipped-beam, high-beam, front fog lamps, reversing lights, registration plate light
REDRear tail lights, rear fog lamps, brake lights — NEVER at the front
YELLOW / AMBERIndicators, side repeaters
WHITE or YELLOW (selective)Front fog lamps
BLUE (flashing)Priority emergency vehicles ONLY (police, fire, ambulance — V-1)

Complete lighting system at a glance

Position (sidelights)
White front / Red rear / Yellow side repeaters. Show width & presence. Activate automatically with dipped/high/fog.
Parking lights
Replaces sidelights when parked in a built-up area. 2 white front + 2 red rear.
Retro-reflectors
Reflect light passively. Triangular shape ONLY for rear trailers/semi-trailers.
Dipped-beam
White. Mandatory sunset–sunrise + tunnels any time + poor visibility + motorcycles always.
High-beam
White. Only poorly-lit interurban roads at night or at ≤40 km/h on any road. Forbidden in urban areas and when stationary.
Rear fog lamps
Red. Mandatory (1 or 2). Only in especially severe conditions. Does NOT illuminate road.
Hazard lights
All indicators flashing. Mandatory cars; optional motorcycles. Use in emergency whether stationary or moving.
Brake lights
Red, at the back. 2 red lights (cars, trailers, mopeds). Third brake light calls extra attention.
Reversing lights
White. Mandatory for cars (not motorcycles). Auto-activates with reverse gear.
Position Lights, Parking Lights & Retro-reflectors

When MUST position lights be on?

  • Between sunset and sunrise on any road
  • When stationary on the carriageway or hard shoulder of ANY road (tunnels, subways, indoor areas)
  • When meteorological/environmental conditions compromise visibility
  • Parked on hard shoulder NOT fit for traffic of an interurban road
  • Parked on carriageway/shoulder of a poorly-illuminated crossing
  • Parked on urban carriageway where vehicle is NOT well perceived at close distance
Poorly illuminated street definition

Plate numbers cannot be read at more than 10 metres away AND dark vehicles are not perceptible at 50 metres. Both conditions must apply.

Dipped-Beam Headlights — Luces de Cruce
White colour. Purpose: illuminate road WITHOUT blinding other users.

Must always be used together with sidelights. Motorcycles: dipped-beam ALWAYS on any public road, day and night.

When MUST dipped-beam be used?

SituationTime
ALL town roads (urban, crossings, tunnels) — regardless of street lightingBetween sunset and sunrise
Interurban roads — fairly illuminated sectionsBetween sunset and sunrise
Tunnels, subways, and road sections with S-5 tunnel signANY time of day
Motorcycles — any public roadANY time of day
Meteorological/environmental conditions significantly reducing visibilityANY time of day
Reversible lanes, contra-flow lanes, adjacent with-flow lanes, additional lanesDay and night — mandatory
The 40 km/h Rule

Vehicles at NOT over 40 km/h may use EITHER dipped OR high-beam on ANY road at any time of day.

High-Beam Headlights — Luces de Carretera
⛔ High-beam FORBIDDEN in:
  • Stationary or parked vehicles (at all times)
  • When travelling in urban areas (speeds over 40 km/h)
  • Whenever there is any possibility of dazzling another road user

When MAY high-beam be used?

  • Between sunset and sunrise on poorly illuminated INTERURBAN roads — at speed over 40 km/h
  • In poorly illuminated tunnels/subways — at speed over 40 km/h
  • Any road — at speed NOT over 40 km/h (either dipped or high-beam)
Cars — number of high-beam headlampsIn pairs (2 headlamps)
Motorcycles / three-wheeled vehicles1 or 2 headlamps
Vehicle without high-beam fittedMust use dipped-beam in all situations requiring high-beam
High-beam illumination range≥ 100 metres ahead
Dipped-beam illumination range≥ 40 metres ahead (hence the 40 km/h rule)
Fog Lamps — Luces Antiniebla
🔦
Front fog lamps

Colour: WHITE or SELECTIVE YELLOW. OPTIONAL (not mandatory). In pairs. Used together with sidelights. Can be used alone (but may dazzle — not advisable).

When to use: fog · heavy rain · snow · dust or smoke clouds · road narrows <6.5 m with warning signs.

🔴
Rear fog lamps

Colour: RED. MANDATORY (singly or in pairs). Does NOT illuminate road — enhances rear conspicuity only. Used with dipped/high-beam or front fog lamps.

Only in especially severe: THICK fog · VERY heavy rain · HEAVY snow · dense dust/smoke clouds.

⛔ Rear fog lamps misuse

Using them in ordinary rain or light fog is prohibited and dazzles following drivers. This is a sanctionable offence.

Fog lamp usage summary by time of day

DAYTIME — adverse visibility conditions
At least sidelights ON + either fog lamps, dipped-beam, or high-beam ON.
NIGHT-TIME — adverse visibility conditions
At least sidelights AND dipped-beam ON. Fog or high-beam: optional.

Other Lights — DRL, Interior, Registration Plate

Daytime Running Lights (DRL)

⚠️ Key exam trap — DRL do NOT illuminate the rear

DRL are white front lights that auto-activate with the engine. They do NOT activate rear lights. In fog or reduced visibility, the driver MUST switch on proper dipped-beam headlights — DRL alone leave the rear of the vehicle completely unlit.

Mandatory since 2011 onAll new passenger cars, vans, and light commercial vehicles sold in the EU
ActivationAutomatically when engine starts; automatically deactivated/dimmed when dipped-beam is activated
Do they replace headlights?NO — DRL are for daytime conspicuity only. Dipped-beam required for all poor visibility and night situations.

Interior & Registration Plate Lights

Interior lights — mandatory forPublic passenger service vehicles AND rental vehicles with driver
Interior lights at nightCan make forward vision difficult (windscreen reflections)
Registration plate lightAuto-illuminates when sidelights are on. SP signal also auto-illuminates when applicable.

Technical Requirements

  • Lights in pairs: same colour + positioned symmetrically
  • No steady light may emit flashing or variable-intensity glare (except as regulated)
  • Number of headlamps cannot exceed the authorised amount
  • Decorative painting, extra lighting or unauthorised reflective devices: FORBIDDEN
Glare & Dazzle Rules
If YOU are dazzled — do NOT pull onto the hard shoulder!

Slow down as far as possible or come to a complete stop. There is a risk of leaving the road or meeting other users on the shoulder.

When to switch from high-beam to dipped-beam

SituationAction
Any possibility of dazzling another road userSwitch to dipped-beam IMMEDIATELY
Bend to the RIGHTSwitch to dipped-beam FIRST — your lights project directly at oncoming drivers. Drivers on left bends may delay switching slightly.
Urban area (speed >40 km/h)High-beam FORBIDDEN — dipped-beam mandatory
Stationary or parked vehicleHigh-beam FORBIDDEN at all times
During overtake — both vehicles alongsideHigh-beam appropriate for overtaking vehicle. Overtaken vehicle must switch to dipped-beam.
Headlamp adjustment obligation

Heavy rear cargo tilts headlamps upward — even dipped-beam can dazzle. The driver must ensure headlamps are correctly adjusted for any load. This is a continuous obligation.
