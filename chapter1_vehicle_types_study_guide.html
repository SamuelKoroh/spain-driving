Overview & Basic Definitions

Foundational concepts for every road user – these definitions are frequently tested.

Key Road Users

Driver (Conductor)
Any person who operates and controls a vehicle on public roads, or is in charge of animals, following traffic laws and ensuring safety. The driver holds full responsibility for vehicle control at all times.
Pedestrian (Peatón)
Any person who moves on foot on public roads. Also includes persons who: push/pull a baby carriage, wheelchair or small non-motorised vehicle; walk while pushing or holding a bicycle or two-wheeled moped; use a wheelchair or personal mobility device at walking speed.
Vehicle Owner (Titular del Vehículo)
Person registered as the vehicle owner in the official Vehicle Register (Registro de Vehículos). Must keep ownership documents in the vehicle and present them to authorities on request. The owner is legally responsible for ensuring only licensed drivers operate the vehicle.
Main Driver (Conductor Habitual)
Person who normally drives a vehicle, even if not the owner. Must hold the appropriate driving licence for that vehicle category. The vehicle owner authorises the main driver and is responsible for this designation.

What is a Vehicle?

🚗 Vehicle (Vehículo)

Any apparatus capable of moving on public or private roads. Vehicles may transport people, animals, goods, or equipment, and can be motorised or non-motorised.

⚙️ Motor Vehicle

Powered by an engine, designed for road use. Excludes: mopeds, trams, and vehicles for people with reduced mobility.

🚘 Automobile

A motor vehicle for transporting people, goods, or both. Can tow other vehicles. Excludes special purpose vehicles.

🔧 Special Vehicle

Designed for specific functions: construction, agriculture, or public services. Can be self-propelled or towed.

Non-Motor Vehicles (Vehículos sin motor)

Cycle (Ciclo)
Manually powered vehicle with at least 2 wheels moved by pedals or cranks. A bicycle is the most common type. E-bikes (Pedelecs/EPAC) that assist up to 25 km/h with motor ≤250W are legally treated as cycles.
Bicycle (Bicicleta)
Standard two-wheeled cycle. Electrically-assisted bicycles (VAE) with motor ≤250W assisting up to 25 km/h are classed as bicycles. Those exceeding these limits become mopeds/motorcycles.
Animal Traction Vehicle (VTA)
Cart or carriage pulled by animals (horses, oxen, donkeys, etc.). Driver must be an adult. Must use the right-hand shoulder. At night: white/yellow lights forward, red lights rearward.
Trailer (Remolque)
Vehicle designed to be towed by a motor vehicle, used for transporting goods or persons. Cannot move independently.
Semi-trailer (Semirremolque)
A trailer that supports part of its own weight on the towing vehicle (tractor unit). The coupling supports vertical load.
Caravan (Caravana)
A towed vehicle built to provide living accommodation while stationary. Not designed for passenger transport while moving on public roads.

The four-wheeled version is called a light quadricycle (cuadriciclo ligero). Requires licence AM (from age 15). Mopeds are not classified as motor vehicles under the Spanish Traffic Law (RGC).

Light Quadricycle vs Motor Quadricycle
  • Light quadricycle: ≤50cc or ≤4 kW electric; ≤45 km/h; unladen mass ≤350 kg. Needs AM licence.
  • Motor quadricycle (heavy): Petrol engine ≤15 kW; electric ≤15 kW; unladen mass ≤400 kg (passenger) or 550 kg (goods). Needs B1 or B licence.

Vehicles for Reduced Mobility (VMP – Vehículo de Movilidad Personal)

Specially designed vehicles for people with disabilities or reduced mobility. This category also includes modern personal mobility devices (electric scooters, segways, hoverboards) governed separately since 2022.


Traditional reduced-mobility vehicles:
  • Electrically powered wheelchairs
  • Small electric mobility scooters for disabled persons
  • Can use pavements and cycle lanes depending on speed
Personal Mobility Vehicles (VMP):
  • Electric scooters, segways, e-unicycles
  • Max speed 25 km/h (most common)
  • Not permitted on motorways or pavements
  • Cannot carry passengers

Trams (Tranvías)

🚋 Tram

Rail vehicles operating along tracks embedded in city streets. A form of public urban transport. Key points: trams are NOT classified as motor vehicles; they always have right of way over road vehicles; drivers must give way to trams when the tram could be endangered.

Vehicle Combinations (Trenes de Carretera)

🚛 Road Train (Tren de carretera)

A motor vehicle towing one or more full trailers. The trailer(s) have their own axles front and back. Requires additional skills for stability and braking distances. Needs B+E, C+E, or equivalent licence.

🚛 Articulated Vehicle (Vehículo articulado)

A tractor unit (cabeza tractora) pulling a semi-trailer. The semi-trailer's front rests on the tractor's fifth-wheel coupling. Better manoeuvrability than a road train. Requires C+E licence.

Key difference

Trailer: all axles are its own, towbar connects at rear of towing vehicle. Semi-trailer: front of semi-trailer rests on fifth wheel of tractor unit – part of the weight is borne by the tractor.

Special Motor Vehicles

Vehicles used for agriculture, construction, public service and leisure – all testable in the exam.

Agricultural Vehicles (Vehículos agrícolas)

🚜 Agricultural Tractor (Tractor agrícola)

Used primarily in farming. Capable of towing heavy agricultural machinery, trailers and implements. Often fitted with attachments (front loader, plough, etc.). Requires licence for agricultural machinery.

🌾 Motorised Agricultural Machine

Purpose-built agricultural machines designed to perform tasks in the field: combine harvesters, cultivators, etc. May require escort vehicles on public roads due to size.

Service & Construction Vehicles

🚧 Self-Propelled Service Machine

Purpose-built public service vehicles. Examples: street sweepers (barredoras), snow ploughs (quitanieves), road maintenance machines. Perform specific operational or maintenance tasks.

🏗️ Self-Propelled Construction Machine

Motorised vehicle with two or more axles, designed to perform construction tasks or heavy work. Examples: cranes, excavators with wheels, road rollers, asphalt layers.

🚧 Construction Backhoe (Retroexcavadora)

Motorised vehicle with at least two axles, designed to move, tow, or push work equipment, machinery, or other construction vehicles. Distinct from a self-propelled construction machine.

🔧 Special Towed Machinery

Agricultural or construction equipment designed to be towed (not self-propelled). Examples: towed agricultural ploughs, towed compactors.

All-Terrain & Leisure Vehicles

🏎️ Quad / ATV (Quad – Todo Terreno)

Equipped with a traction system suitable for its purpose, four or more wheels, and a handlebar (not a steering wheel). Maximum speed may be limited by technical features or intended use. Classified as a special vehicle, not a standard automobile.

🚂 Tourist Train (Tren turístico)

A tractor unit with trailers built to carry passengers for sightseeing or leisure purposes. Has a limited maximum speed. Subject to specific traffic restrictions. Used in city centres, tourist areas, and theme parks.

Remember

A tourist train is technically a tractor unit + trailers combination, NOT a railway vehicle. It is classified as a special vehicle subject to specific traffic regulations.

Vehicle Mass & Weight Terms

Weight terminology is extensively tested. Know every term precisely.

Core Weight Definitions

Tare Weight / Unladen Mass (Masa en orden de marcha)
Weight of the vehicle complete with standard equipment, ready to operate: fuel, lubricants, coolant, tools, spare wheel. Does NOT include passengers, cargo, or driver.
Tare Weight in Motion (Masa en circulación)
Tare weight (unladen mass) PLUS the weight of the driver (standard figure of 75 kg is used). This is the minimum operating weight.
Gross Vehicle Weight (Masa total)
The actual total weight of the vehicle as used at any given moment: tare + all passengers + all cargo + any loaded fuel. The actual weight during operation.
Maximum Authorised Mass – MAM (Masa Máxima Autorizada – MMA)
The legal maximum total mass permitted for the vehicle while in use on public roads. Set by the manufacturer and confirmed by the authorities in the technical inspection. THIS is the key figure for licence requirements.
Maximum Authorised Mass of the Combination (MMAC)
The highest total weight a loaded combination (vehicle + trailer/semi-trailer) is allowed to have when travelling on public roads.
Axle Load (Carga por eje)
The weight supported by a single axle of the vehicle. Legal limits exist for each axle type to protect road surfaces. Standard limit: 10 tonnes per single axle; 18 t for tandem axle.
Weight Per Axle (Peso por eje)
The amount of weight that a single axle transmits to the ground through its wheels. Related to road damage and legal compliance.

Why Weight Matters for Licence B

Licence B weight limits at a glance

  • Vehicle MAM: ≤ 3,500 kg (drives alone with B licence)
  • Passengers: ≤ 8 seats (plus driver = 9 total)
  • Trailer (O1): ≤ 750 kg MAM → can tow with just B licence
  • Trailer >750 kg but combination total ≤3,500 kg → still B licence
  • Combination MAM >3,500 kg → requires B+E licence

Licence B – The Complete Guide

Everything a B-licence holder must know about which vehicles they can legally drive.

What Licence B allows you to drive

  • Motor vehicles with MAM ≤ 3,500 kg
  • With maximum 8 passenger seats (+ driver)
  • Passenger cars (turismos), vans (N1 category), mixed-use vehicles
  • Motor quadricycles (heavy quadricycles, unladen ≤400 kg passenger / ≤550 kg goods)
  • Towing a trailer ≤750 kg MAM (combined total can still be ≤3,500 kg)
  • If combined mass of vehicle + trailer ≤3,500 kg → no additional licence needed
  • Ambulances ≤3,500 kg MAM

Vehicles NOT covered by Licence B

Requires additional licence
  • Vehicles with MAM >3,500 kg → C1 (up to 7,500 kg) or C (above)
  • Buses >8 passenger seats → D1 (minibuses) or D (buses)
  • B+E: needed when towing a trailer and combined MAM >3,500 kg
  • Motorcycles (>50cc, >45 km/h) → A1, A2, or A licence
  • Mopeds → AM licence (though B includes AM permissions in Spain after licence reform)

Special Licence B Conditions in Spain

Condition Details
Minimum age 18 years (17 years for accompanied driving – "conducción acompañada")
Provisional period First 2 years as novice driver (conductor novel): alcohol limit 0.15 mg/l breath (0.3 g/l blood); lower speed limits apply on some roads
Points system New drivers start with 8 points; reach 12 after 3 years without offences. B-licence can be revalidated every 10 years (standard) or less frequently.
Licence validity Renewed every 10 years until age 65; every 5 years from age 65; every 2 years from age 70
Medical fitness Required at each renewal; specific conditions may restrict licence to certain adaptations (coded licence restrictions)
Speed limits for novice drivers Motorways/dual carriageways: 100 km/h (vs 120/130 for experienced); conventional roads: 90 km/h (vs 100) during first year
Accompanied driving (CAP) From age 17 with experienced driver (≥3 years, ≥27 years old) present. On reaching 18, standard B licence is issued without additional test.

Towing Rules with Licence B

🔗 Towing with Licence B
Allowed (B licence):
  • Trailer ≤750 kg MAM (any combination weight)
  • Trailer >750 kg if combined total ≤3,500 kg
  • Single axle trailer or twin-axle trailer within limits
  • Caravan within weight limits
Requires B+E:
  • Trailer >750 kg and combined total >3,500 kg
  • Heavy caravan (combined >3,500 kg)
  • Semi-trailer combinations
