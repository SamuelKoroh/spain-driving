General Rules of Behaviour

All road users have specific obligations and prohibitions under Art. 9–11 RGC.

Driver Obligations

Control at all times
Adjust speed and movements to road, weather and traffic conditions.
Vulnerable users
Exercise necessary caution when approaching children, elderly, disabled, and blind individuals.
Freedom of movement
Maintain full freedom of movement and unobstructed field of vision. Keep suitable posture.
Refuelling
Switch off engine, lights, radio, and ALL electromagnetic devices (including mobile phones) when refuelling.

Strict Prohibitions

⛔ Forbidden for all drivers
  • Throwing/abandoning objects that could cause fires, pollution or road hazards
  • Driving negligently or recklessly
  • Using mobile phones, headphones while driving (except fully hands-free)
  • Using radar detectors or devices intended to evade traffic surveillance
  • Internet/TV/DVD while in motion (GPS cannot be operated while moving)

Vehicle Owner Obligations

Keep vehicle in perfect conditionCarry out all adequate examinations and inspections (ITV)
Prevent unlicensed drivingMust prevent those without a valid licence from driving the vehicle
Facilitate identificationMust facilitate driver's identification to authorities in event of infraction
Register regular driverDisclose regular driver's name to DGT (Registro de Vehículos) to transfer obligations

Essential definitions for understanding lane rules and driving obligations.

Road Types by Features

Motorway (Autopista)
Separate carriageways per direction. No level crossings. No adjoining property access. May be free or toll. Min access speed: 60 km/h.
Dual carriageway (Autovía)
Separate carriageways, median strips. No grade intersections. No bordering property access.
Conventional highway
Any main road not meeting motorway/dual carriageway definition. No use limitations.
Single carriageway road
Reserved exclusively for motorcar driving. One carriageway, no adjoining property access.
Cannot use motorways/dual carriageways

Cycles, mopeds, pedestrians, animals, animal-drawn vehicles, and PMVs. Exception: cyclists over 14 may use hard shoulders unless prohibited by sign.

Driving Rules — Lane Usage

Rules for lane selection vary by road type and location (urban vs interurban).

General rule

Drive on the carriageway, not the hard shoulder (except emergencies). On bends and humps: keep close to right edge, keep side gap, leave opposing lane completely free.

Dual carriageways — inside or outside urban areas

SituationRule
2 lanes (separated or not)Drive in the right lane
3 lanes (broken lines)Right lane always. Centre only to overtake or turn left. Left lane NEVER.

Interurban — single carriageway, 3+ lanes same direction

Heavy vehicles rule

Lorries, minivans, special vehicles >3,500 kg MAM, and combinations >7 m must use the RIGHT lane only. Adjacent lane exceptionally allowed. All other lanes NEVER.

Inside urban areas

SituationRule
2+ lanes with longitudinal markingsMost suitable lane for destination. Only abandon to change direction, overtake, stop, or park.
No longitudinal markingsRight lane
Motorways through urban groundInterurban rules apply
⛔ Absolutely forbidden

Driving on pavements and pedestrian zones. Exceptions by regulation only for PMVs, bicycles, and cycles.

Right of Way — Priority Rules
Order of authority at junctions

Agents > Light signals > Vertical signs > Road markings. Without signs: yield to vehicles from the RIGHT.

Exceptions — Priority Regardless of Direction

1. Paved over unpaved road
Vehicles on a paved road have priority over those on an unpaved road.
2. Already in roundabout
Vehicles already circulating inside a roundabout have priority.
3. Motorways / dual carriageways
Vehicles on these roads have priority over those wanting to join.
4. Public road over private path
Vehicles on a public road have priority over those from a private path.
Rail vehicles — always priority

Trains and trams ALWAYS have priority over all road users. Forbidden to cross a level crossing when barriers are closed, going up or down, or when traffic lights forbid it.
